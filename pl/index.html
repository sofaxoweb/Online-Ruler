What is an online ruler?

An online ruler is a web-based measuring tool that displays a ruler on your screen at real physical size. It calculates your display's pixels-per-inch (PPI) and draws every centimetre, millimetre and inch to match a physical ruler held against the screen. After a one-time calibration, you can measure small objects directly on your display — no app to install.

How this ruler measures in real size

Your screen does not know what an inch is — it only knows pixels. The bridge between the two is pixel density, measured in PPI (pixels per inch). If your screen shows 141 pixels per inch, the ruler draws one inch as exactly 141 pixels, one centimetre as 55.6 pixels, and one millimetre as 5.56 pixels.

There is a catch: two monitors can share the same 1920×1080 resolution yet be 22 or 32 inches wide — the same pixels, very different real sizes. That is why an online ruler must learn your screen's true PPI. TrueRuler does this in two ways: it recognises common phones, tablets and laptops from a device database and applies their factory PPI, or it computes PPI from the screen's diagonal size.

One more detail: browsers work in CSS pixels, which can differ from physical pixels on high-density ("Retina") displays. TrueRuler reads your device's pixel ratio and converts automatically, and it warns you if browser zoom is not at 100%, because zooming changes the size of a CSS pixel.

The calibration formula

When you enter your screen's diagonal size, the ruler computes PPI with the Pythagorean theorem:

PPI = √(width² + height²) ÷ diagonal inches

Worked example. A 15.6-inch laptop with a 1920×1080 screen: √(1920² + 1080²) = √4,852,800 ≈ 2202.9 pixels diagonally. Divide by 15.6 inches → 141.2 PPI. One inch is therefore drawn as 141.2 pixels, one centimetre as 55.6 pixels (141.2 ÷ 2.54), and one millimetre as 5.56 pixels.

How to use TrueRuler

  1. Check the status dot. If your device is recognised, the dot turns green and the ruler is already true to size.
  2. Calibrate if needed. Enter your screen's diagonal for instant calibration — or open the full options: pick your device from the list, or hold a bank card (85.60 mm wide) against the blue guide and drag the slider until they match.
  3. Choose your unit. Switch between centimetres, millimetres and inches in the toolbar above the ruler.
  4. Measure. Hold the object against the ruler edge, press Measure and drag a box over anything on screen, or open the Protractor to measure angles.
  5. Refine and reuse. Nudge the measure box with your arrow keys, copy the reading, and come back any time — your calibration is saved on this device.

Vertical ruler and screen rotation

Press Rotate to swap the horizontal and vertical rulers — handy for measuring height or when your screen is narrow. The vertical ruler works exactly like the horizontal one, in centimetres, millimetres and inches.

Measuring objects with the drag-to-measure box

Press Measure and drag a box over anything visible on screen — a photo, a diagram, a product image. The box shows live width × height in cm, mm and inches. Drag the handles to resize, drag the centre to move it, and use your arrow keys for precise nudging. Copy puts the reading on your clipboard.

Measuring ring size

To find your ring size, wrap a thin strip of paper around your finger, mark where it overlaps, then lay the strip flat against the on-screen ruler and read the length in millimetres — most jewellers size rings from the finger's circumference in mm. For a ring you already own, open the Measure box and drag it across the ring's inner diameter. Finger size shifts slightly during the day, so measure in the evening for the most reliable result.

Protractor mode

Press Protractor to overlay a full 180° protractor. Drag either arm to the angle you want to measure; the readout shows the degrees live. Drag the centre to move the whole protractor. It uses the same calibration as the ruler, so angles stay true on any screen.

Printable paper ruler

Press Print for a paper ruler at exact true size: 18 cm on one edge and 7 inches on the other. Print at 100% scale (never "fit to page"), cut along the edge, and check it against a known object. A printed ruler is handy when you need to measure something away from the screen.

Limitations

  • Browser zoom must be 100%. Zooming changes pixel sizes; TrueRuler warns you if zoom is off.
  • Operating-system display scaling matters. If your OS scales the display (for example 125% on Windows), recalibrate after changing it.
  • External monitors vary. The same resolution can mean different physical sizes, so calibrate a new monitor instead of trusting the old setting.
  • Phones in desktop mode. Some mobile browsers report an unusual viewport; if the ruler looks wrong, use the bank-card calibration.
  • Not a PD ruler. This is a general-purpose measuring ruler, not a pupillary-distance ruler for ordering glasses — that needs a specialised tool.
  • Screen length limits. The on-screen ruler can only be as long as your display; for longer objects, use the printable ruler or measure in sections.

Frequently asked questions

Is this online ruler accurate?

Yes — once calibrated, it is accurate to about a millimetre on most screens. The green status dot means your device was recognised and the factory pixel density is applied. If your device is not recognised, calibrate with the bank-card method, which is the most precise option.

How do I calibrate my screen?

Pick any of three methods: choose your device from the list, enter your screen's diagonal size, or hold a standard bank card (85.60 mm wide) against the blue guide and drag the slider until they match. Your calibration is saved automatically.

Why does the ruler look wrong on my monitor?

Almost always because two monitors share a resolution but not a physical size — a 1920×1080 panel can be 22 or 32 inches. The ruler cannot guess, so calibrate that monitor once with the bank-card method and it will stay correct.

How long is the on-screen ruler?

As long as your screen — a 12-inch ruler fits on most laptops, while phones show less. For a fixed-length reference, print the paper ruler: exactly 18 cm on one edge and 7 inches on the other at 100% scale.

Can I use it as a millimetre ruler?

Yes. Switch the toolbar to mm and the rulers redraw with millimetre ticks and numbering — ideal for precise small measurements.

Does it work on phones and tablets?

Yes. The layout adapts to small screens, and most popular phones and tablets are in the device database for automatic calibration. On touch screens, drag the measure box with your finger.

Do I need my camera?

No. Unlike AR measuring apps, TrueRuler never uses your camera — it measures from your screen's pixel density, so there is nothing to point and no permission to grant.

Is it really free?

Yes, completely free with no account and no installation. All processing happens in your browser; nothing you measure is uploaded anywhere.

Can I measure my ring size with it?

Yes. Wrap a paper strip around your finger, mark the overlap, lay it flat against the on-screen ruler and read the millimetres — jewellers size rings from finger circumference. Or drag the Measure box across a ring's inner diameter.

How do I print a ruler at actual size?

Press Print, then print at 100% scale — never "fit to page", which would shrink it. Cut along the edge and verify against a bank card (85.60 mm) before trusting it.

Does it save my settings?

Your calibration, unit choice and theme are saved in your browser's local storage on your own device. Clearing site data resets them; nothing is sent to any server.

What is PPI and why does it matter?

PPI means pixels per inch — your screen's pixel density. A ruler must know it to convert pixels into real centimetres and inches. That single number is the whole difference between a decorative ruler image and a true-size measuring tool.
